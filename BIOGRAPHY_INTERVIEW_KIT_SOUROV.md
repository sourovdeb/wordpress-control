## PART B — HOW THESE QUESTIONS ARE BUILT (method, with sources)

Six findings drive the design. They are not decoration; each changes how a question is phrased.

1. **Memory is hierarchical and cue-driven** (Conway & Pleydell-Pearce, *Self-Memory System*, 2000; Conway 2005). Recall runs from lifetime-period → general-event → event-specific sensory detail. A concrete sensory or contextual cue triggers *direct* retrieval (bottom-up); a vague theme forces slow *generative* search. → **Every question leads with a concrete anchor** (a smell, a sound, an object, the weather, who was in the room), not a theme.

2. **Overgeneral Memory (OGM)** (Williams & Broadbent 1986; Williams et al. 2007, *CaR-FA-X* model). Depression + PTSD + childhood sexual-abuse history — all three in this profile — make a person retrieve **categories** ("tension was constant," "she beat me") instead of **single episodes**. Crucially, OGM is **worse for positive memories**. → **Every question demands ONE event, at one place, on one day, lasting under a day** (the clinical specificity criterion), and the set **over-samples positive episodes** because they are the hardest and the most absent from the record.

3. **The reminiscence bump** (Rubin; Berntsen & Rubin; PLOS One systematic review 2018). Most self-defining memories cluster at **ages ~10–30**, especially **first-times and transitions**; immigrants show a **second bump at the age of immigration**. → The set is **weighted toward 10–30 and toward his two emigrations** (Bangladesh→Sydney; Australia→France/Réunion) and toward "first time" framing.

4. **Memory is reconstructive — people invent, modify, delete** (Loftus, misinformation effect, 1974–2005; Schacter, reconstruction; *"Lost in the Mall"*). Each retrieval can rewrite the trace; schemas fill gaps invisibly. A **2024 study found AI interviewers amplify false memories through leading questions.** → Questions are **non-leading** (no presupposed content; open *what/where/who/how*, never "did you feel X?"), and **"I don't remember" / "I'm not sure that's real" are explicitly valid answers**. Two prompts deliberately surface the invented/borrowed layer (family-told stories; memories that changed across tellings).

5. **The Cognitive Interview** (Fisher & Geiselman 1992) — the forensic protocol, and a natural fit for the self-named "Forensic Auditor." Four mnemonics, of which **context reinstatement + report-everything** are highest-yield and **anti-confabulation** (the "do not guess" instruction is built in): (a) **reinstate context** — return to the scene, weather, body-state; (b) **report everything**, trivial included; (c) **change order** — recall backwards or from a midpoint; (d) **change perspective** — describe it as another person saw it. → Used as the engine for stuck or over-rehearsed memories.

6. **The Life Story Interview** (McAdams 2007, *LSI-II*) — the academic protocol the attached Past-Authoring suite derives from: life **chapters**, key **scenes** (high point, low point, turning point, vivid memory, wisdom event, spiritual experience), **future script**, and **ideology/values**. Framed as: *"parts of the past as you remember them… the story is selective… there are no right or wrong answers."* → Set 2 uses this structure; **the raw episode is captured first, the meaning second**, so interpretation never contaminates the detail.

**Operating rules for whoever runs the interview (you, or Claude):**
- One cue per question. Wait. Do not stack.
- Never supply a detail he didn't say. If he stalls, reinstate context ("where were you standing? what could you hear?") — do not offer a guess to confirm.
- "I don't remember" is a complete answer. Mark the gap; move on.
- Capture the event **before** asking what it meant.
- Date every session. If a later telling differs, **log both — the change is data, not an error** (this matches the biography's existing method note).

## PART D — SET 2: SELF-AUTHORING EPOCH FRAMEWORK
*Built on the attached Past-Authoring templates (7 epochs; ≥4 experiences each; event → effects → analysis), augmented with McAdams key-scenes and the cueing rules. This version does not feed you facts — it gives the scaffold and lets you generate the experiences. The seven epochs are drawn from your own documented chronology so the structure fits your life, not a template.*

### Your seven epochs (proposed — adjust freely)
1. **Chittagong, the early years** (≈1986–1998) — village to town, three schools.
2. **The adult-child** (≈12–18) — water, mediator, the books, music school, what was done.
3. **Sydney: the kite** (≈2005–2014) — arrival, survival, collapse, addiction.
4. **Sydney: the rebuild** (≈2014–2017) — craft, sobriety, Vanessa, the decision to leave.
5. **France & the new language** (≈2017–2023) — marriage, Paris, becoming French.
6. **Réunion: the three blows & the door** (2023–2025) — father's death, the boss, the daughter, diagnosis, therapy.
7. **The Forensic Auditor** (2025–now) — CELTA, the fight, the practice, stability, advocacy.

### Per-epoch protocol (run for each of the seven)
For each epoch, generate **at least four experiences** using these four cue-types — at least one must be **positive**:

- **A. A single day.** Not "what those years were like" — one day you can place in time and space. Start with: where were you standing, what could you hear, who was there?
- **B. A first time.** The first time something happened in that epoch (first job, first kiss, first loss, first time you felt safe/unsafe).
- **C. An object or a smell.** Name one object, food, song, or smell from that epoch, then follow it to the single scene it pulls up.
- **D. A forced positive.** One good moment from that epoch — however small. *(If you can't find one, write "none I can retrieve" — that absence is itself a finding.)*

Then, for **each** experience, the three layers from the attachment — **event first, meaning second**:
- **Event only:** what happened. No interpretation yet.
- **Effect on others / the world:** how it changed your view of people, of how the world works.
- **Effect on you:** what it did to your trust, your hope, your sense of your own value, your personality.

### Cross-epoch key scenes (McAdams LSI — answer once each, any epoch)
- **High point:** the single best moment of your life so far — one scene, fully reinstated.
- **Low point:** the worst — only as much as is safe to revisit; you may name it without entering it.
- **Turning point:** one episode where you changed direction or changed how you saw yourself.
- **Positive childhood memory:** one specific good scene before age 12.
- **Negative childhood memory:** one specific scene — pattern level is fine; you set the depth.
- **Vivid adult memory:** any single adult scene that stays sharp for no obvious reason. (These are often the truest.)
- **Wisdom event:** one time you acted wisely, or learned something you still use.
- **Spiritual / mystical:** one experience that felt larger than ordinary, if any.

### Future script (the part the past exists to serve)
- **The next chapter:** what is the title and the opening scene of the chapter starting now?
- **Hopes & plans:** one concrete thing you intend to have done in five years — described as a scene.
- **The life project:** the single thing this whole life is *for*. State it in one sentence.

### Reconstruction prompts (the invent/modify/delete layer — answer last)
- A **family-told story** about you that you "remember" but suspect you only know because you were told. Which is it?
- A memory that has **changed across tellings** — you've noticed it isn't the same as it used to be. What moved?
- One thing you are **genuinely unsure actually happened** the way you say it. Leave it uncertain on the page.

## PART E — CAPTURE & SAFETY

**Capture format (for reuse in papers/blog/YouTube):** record each answer as `EPOCH · EXPERIENCE TITLE · DATE OF TELLING · [event] / [effect] / [uncertainty flag]`. Keep the event verbatim. This is the same documentary discipline the existing biography uses.

**Pacing.** The Past-Authoring suite itself warns: this is reverie work, not a sprint; **do one epoch per sitting**, sleep between sessions (consolidation aids it), stop if flooded. Given the clinical picture on file (C-PTSD, bipolar I, insomnia), the strongest material is also the most destabilising — keep the rhythm rules from your own Stability Program, and route the heaviest scenes near sessions with Dr Padovani, not late at night.

**On truth.** The goal is not a perfect record of the past — that does not exist for anyone. The goal is the version you can tell now, dated and honest about its own gaps. That is more defensible, and more useful, than a smooth story.

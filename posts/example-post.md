## Why Pronunciation Is Often Neglected

Many teachers avoid teaching pronunciation because it feels technical or elusive.
Yet learners consistently rate pronunciation as one of their biggest barriers to communication.

## Segmentals vs Suprasegmentals

**Segmentals** are individual sounds — consonants and vowels.
**Suprasegmentals** are the music of language: stress, rhythm, intonation, and connected speech.

Research shows that suprasegmental errors cause more communication breakdown than sound-level errors.
A learner who says "I LIVE in Paris" with wrong word stress is harder to understand than one who mispronounces a vowel.

## Practical Classroom Activities

- **Minimal pairs drilling** — ship/sheep, live/leave, bit/beat
- **Stress tapping** — tap on stressed syllables while speaking
- **Shadowing** — listen and repeat simultaneously with a native speaker recording
- **Transcription tasks** — write what you hear, then compare to the script

## The Phonemic Chart

The IPA phonemic chart is a tool, not a destination.
Use it to help learners self-correct and use dictionaries independently.
Spend 5 minutes per lesson on a single sound in context rather than drilling in isolation.

## Common Teacher Mistakes

1. Treating pronunciation as a separate slot rather than integrating it throughout lessons
2. Only correcting segmental errors and ignoring stress/intonation
3. Using the same accent as the only model (expose learners to multiple accents)
4. Overcorrecting — this kills fluency and confidence
